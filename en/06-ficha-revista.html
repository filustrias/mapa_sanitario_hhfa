Revised Form

Health Facility Registration Form
Proposed revised version, with questions written out in full and HHFA indicators annotated

Working version for discussion with the Office of Studies, Planning and Statistics. It keeps the structure, the numbering and the purpose of Form version 19_01, and reformulates the questions and the response options following the wording of the Harmonized Health Facility Assessment, translated from the original.

Every question is written out in full, exactly as it is to be read aloud, with the unit of measure, the reference period and the point of reference built into the text itself. Each one is annotated with the HHFA indicator that it makes computable.

⚠ READ FIRST: the drafting standard adopted

A question that each enumerator has to complete on their own is a different question in every health facility. "Distance to the referral facility — minutes, total km" forces the person collecting the data to decide alone which road, by which means of transport, in which season of the year, and measured how. The answers that come out of it are not comparable with one another, and no subsequent analysis can recover that variation.

This version applies nine drafting rules to every question. They are the same ones the HHFA applies, and the effect is that the question stays identical across all facilities, whoever asks it.

  1. The question is read out in full, exactly as written. The enumerator neither completes nor summarises it.
  2. The unit of measure is inside the question — kilometres, minutes, number of people, square metres.
  3. The reference period is inside the question — today, in the past 7 days, in the past 3 months, in the past 12 months, in the past 2 years.
  4. The point of reference is inside the question — from where to where, counted how, by whom, under what conditions.
  5. One question yields one fact. Composite concepts are split into separate questions.
  6. The response options are mutually exclusive and exhaustive, and they are written on the form, not left to the judgement of whoever is recording the answer.
  7. There is always a code for "does not know" (98) and, where applicable, for "not applicable" (97). A blank field is no longer ambiguous.
  8. Instructions to the enumerator are set apart graphically from the text to be read to the respondent. In this document they appear in italics and in capitals.
  9. Consistency checks are stated alongside the questions they apply to, so that they can be programmed into the data collection application.

The HHFA foresees instruments exactly like the Health Map. The inventory has two subareas — 1.3.2, availability of specific services, and 1.3.3, availability of technologies and procedures — whose titles end with the note "to be used only when the readiness module does not apply". This proposal rests on that route, and the consequence is highly favourable: more than nine in ten of the indicators captured here yield in self-reported mode, requiring neither observation nor any change to the current collection process.

Conventions. The numbering of existing items is retained, so that the historical series can be reconstructed. [MS] marks items specific to the Health Map, with no counterpart in the HHFA, which are kept because they are its own particular contribution — but they too are reformulated, because the drafting problem is independent of where the question came from. ◆ marks the questions that require an enumerator making observations. The lists so flagged still need validation by the national programmes.

What has changed, and why. The current Form has an item 3.2.3 with four fields — minutes, total km, km of paved road, km of dirt track — and no instruction at all. Nothing says which road is to be measured, in which means of transport the minutes are counted, whether the time includes waiting for the ambulance, or what to do when the dirt track becomes impassable in the rains. Three enumerators produce three different answers for the same facility, and the difference between them is indistinguishable from the real difference between facilities.

The six questions above fix: the means of transport (3.2.3, which becomes a datum with value in its own right — a facility that evacuates on foot is a different reality), the road actually travelled rather than the straight line, the breakdown verified by addition, and the two seasons of the year, with a code of its own for the impassable route. It becomes possible to calculate evacuation time in the worst season, which is the information that matters for maternal mortality.

SECTION 7 — Services

The central section of the Form. It rests on subareas 1.3.2 and 1.3.3 of the HHFA — the ones the instrument reserves for measuring availability without a readiness module.

A note on reading this section. Two formats are used here. Stand-alone questions are written out in full. Lists of items — vaccines, tests, contraceptive methods — are set out in a table, which is the HHFA’s own format: the lead-in is read once, in full, and then the items are read one by one. It is an efficient format provided the lead-in is complete, and that is why all the lead-ins below are written out in full.

What this version delivers, and what it leaves for later

345 HHFA indicators, out of the 2,604 in the inventory. The number on its own says less than its composition does: nineteen subareas are completed in full, and they are precisely the availability subareas — what the Health Map Form was designed for. The instrument now produces, in internationally comparable language, exactly what it already set out to measure.

More than nine in ten yield in self-reported mode. Only Section 9 requires observation throughout, and it can be applied only in the facilities that are visited without the form ceasing to work in the others.

The greater gain, however, is not in the number of indicators. It is in the drafting. A question written out in full, with the unit, the period and the point of reference inside it, produces the same answer whoever asks it — and that is what makes two facilities, two provinces and two editions of the Health Map comparable. Several items in this form add no new indicator and are kept here for that reason alone: the distance to the referral facility, the state of repair, the vehicle fleet, the specialties, the general services. They are [MS], they are the Health Map’s own contribution, and they gain in precision without changing their purpose.

Three items are deliberately left unresolved, and it is worth saying so: the pharmacy (7.2.10), sterilization and the distribution of albendazole remain as questions about existence. In all three cases the corresponding HHFA indicator depends on physical verification — medicine within its expiry date, equipment working, product observed — and there is no honest way of producing it in self-reported mode. They stay as they are until the decision on observation is taken.

What is left for the next phase is the readiness dimension: guidelines available, staff trained, medicines and diagnostic means verified. These are the Type 3 and Type 4 increments in document 4, and they all require an enumerator making observations. They are left out of this version not because they matter less — in many cases they matter more — but because adopting them depends on a decision about the mode of collection that has not yet been taken.
